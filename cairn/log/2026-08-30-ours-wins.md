# Ours wins, and the knob is gone

`prefer` outlived its reason. The spec had argued the split plainly: the winner had to be separable from the baseline because authority sat on the replayed side, so a caller needing its own edit judged had to place it there, and without a preference of its own that placement would have cost it every collision. Authority went yesterday, and the argument went with it.

What remained was a field every caller set the same way. tcard, tcal and neverest all passed `Left`; nothing in the ecosystem passed `Right`. Git already names the arrangement and everybody reads it the same way, so the left side is `ours` and wins, the right side is `theirs`, and neither is stated at a call site any more.

A mechanism died quietly with it. `displaces` carried the left-side addition that a winning right-side addition replaced, and it was computed as `applies && both_added`. Once the left side always wins, `applies` is only ever true where the right side's update beats a removal, and a removal is not an addition, so the two conditions became unsatisfiable. The field, `both_added`, and the replace-where-it-stood paths in both the component and the line replay are gone rather than left looking live. The merged calendar keeps the left side's addition and reports the collision, which is what it already did in every reachable case.

Two tests went with the preference, both of which existed only to show it could be flipped: one asserting the right side's value was carried when the right side was preferred, one asserting that stating `Left` gave what saying nothing gave. Three more that looped over both preferences now run once. No law lost coverage.

Capabilities moved: merge.
